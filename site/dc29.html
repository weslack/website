A year on, back on the SOHOplessly Broken range. This round was heavy on reconnaissance: mapping a wall of network storage and lining up attack vectors before committing.

  • 13 hosts mapped
  • 1 device owned
  • 1 subnet (192.168.10.0/24)

We catalogued the subnet host by host: open ports, likely make and model, and a running note of what looked promising. The target list skewed hard toward NAS appliances, each a big attack surface of file-sharing and management services.

Attack surface

NUUO NVR

Owned

ftp · ssh · http · https. The one we opened this round; no pivot attempts made afterward.

Zyxel NSA325 v2

Enumerated

Wide open: ftp · ssh · http · https · smb · ipp and a tor-orport on 9001. The most exposed box on the network.

QNAP NAS

Enumerated

Eighteen open ports, from rsync and nfs to a pair of high unknowns, plenty to work with.

Control4 controller

Enumerated

ssh · http · smb plus the Control4 admd service cluster and VNC, the same family we owned in prior events.

NAS fleet

Enumerated

Drobo 5N2, Netgear ReadyNAS, Seagate NAS, and an Asus RT-N53, all mapped and running the usual SMB / AFP / web management stack.

Vera & D-Link DCS-930L

Enumerated

A home-automation hub behind a wall of filtered ports, and a D-Link camera reachable with a blank admin password.
